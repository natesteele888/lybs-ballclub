Five rules that cover almost everything

1. Nobody stands still.
On every batted ball you are fielding it, covering a base, cutting a throw, or backing up a throw. If you can't say which one, you're wrong.
2. Throws to second: ball side goes out.
Ball to left field, shortstop goes out for the relay and the second baseman covers. Ball to right field, they swap.
3. Throws to third: shortstop is the cut.
Every time, from anywhere. The third baseman stays on the bag.
4. Throws home: left field, third baseman cuts. Center or right, first baseman cuts.
The catcher is the only one who can see the runner, so the catcher makes the call: "Cut!" or "Let it go!"
5. The pitcher backs up one base, not two.
Third or home — pick the one the ball is going to and get deep behind it.

Infield fly

Runners on first and second, or bases loaded, with fewer than two outs, and an infielder can catch a fair pop with ordinary effort — the umpire calls the batter out on the spot. Nobody is forced anymore. So catch it, and if you drop it, do not go running to a base looking for a force that isn't there. The runners can still advance at their own risk, so hold your bag and look them back.

The catcher does not have to catch it cleanly for it to be a strike. He has to catch it in the air. If it touches dirt first, the batter is live under the rules above — and half your team will forget.
